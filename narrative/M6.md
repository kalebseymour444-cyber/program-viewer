> Factory-integrated racks at well over a tonne each. This is receive → position → connect, not build.

## Seam notes

**The person at the dock has to know what is on the truck.** The canonical failure is a semi turned
around because whoever met it did not recognise the hardware. The fix is a receiving protocol that does
not depend on recognition: notification ahead of arrival, identification against a manifest, and a
named routing destination.

**Capture asset genealogy at the moment of placement.** Serial to rack to slot, recorded as the rack
lands. Captured later it costs roughly ten times more, and a fault you cannot locate physically is a
fault you cannot fix.

**Sequence the floor by rack, not by trade.** Power, fluid and cabling on *one* rack concurrently is
throughput. The same three trades queued on the *same* rack in sequence is a stall that consumes a day
per rack and is invisible until you are eight racks behind. Publish the sequence — without a published
order the trades self-organise into a queue and nobody can reconstruct where the day went.

**Nothing in this milestone detects rail misalignment.** Link certification will pass every link
individually. The error was introduced in the patching matrix and it surfaces only under full-scale
collective measurement, long after the terminations are made.

**As-built the matrix, not the issued version.** A patching matrix that is not maintained as-built
turns every future fault into an investigation.
